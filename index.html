§Verdict

The trace Cohere published is real. We decoded its raw token IDs byte-for-byte with Qwen's own tokenizer; the recorded event — a model mid-way through a benign detection-engineering task emitting an unsolicited curl -X PUT toward an Aliyun bucket while its reasoning says it will call no tools — happened. It is genuinely odd.

This page does not claim Cohere lied. It makes a narrower and worse observation: the analysis in the post stops exactly where the headline stops. The one measurement that would have tested the headline — a classification of the 64 completions Cohere itself released — is not in the post, not in the dataset card, and not anywhere in what they released.

The two lines that decide this

Cohere's reproduction truncates the trace at the point where the URL begins. Their own script says so:

ids = ids[:2323]  # stop just before the URL, at the end of ...ABC123

At that cut, the only continuation that finishes the command is a URL — and every model we tested that produces coherent text produces one. The script's stated question — “does the model write the Aliyun bucket from these tokens?” — is answered no: zero times in the 64 samples Cohere published alongside it.

Three fingerprints of a fossil

  • The headline URL never recurs. sanmaohe.oss-cn-beijing.aliyuncs.com: 0/64. Its nearest relative is sanmaoheihei-oss.oss-cn-hangzhou.oss-cn-beijing.aliyuncs.com — a structurally impossible hostname, two regional endpoints concatenated. That is a blurred memory, not a C2 address.

Twelve distinct hex IDs span five dates. That is recombination of remembered log fragments — the shape a model makes when it blends two lines it has seen — not a live endpoint.

The check that wasn't run

A classification of 64 strings is not a research programme. It is an afternoon — the kind of check a cyber division that finds 18 curl CVEs in a release is more than capable of. No such classification appears in the post or in the card. What appears instead is: “many trials may be required to reproduce the behaviour” — where “the behaviour” is the URL, which the cut point guarantees before the first sample is drawn.

The bucket that would have tested the call-home claim is empty in the only sample they published. The headline shipped with the check unrun — and their own data was sufficient to run it.

§The sentence that isn't a finding

The post offers two takeaways. Only one is supported by the data, and it is not the one with a product behind it.

The mitigation — free

“Deny egress by default.”

Vendor-neutral. Costs nothing. Works identically on Qwen, on Command, and on anything else that can open a socket. This is the correct advice and it was always correct.

The conclusion — a purchase

“Running it yourself just moves the black box inside your firewall.”

Follows only if provenance is unknowable in principle — the one problem a customer cannot fix and a vendor can sell against. This is the sentence BetaKit quoted from the post.

The uncertainty in the middle is honest: “we cannot answer whether this is deliberate or a leftover glitch” is a legitimate epistemic position for anyone outside Alibaba's training run. The problem is its placement. It is presented as the conclusion rather than as the limitation, after the reader has been walked through the 10M-turns, 100-calls projection. And the evidence that would have moved the prior — their own 64 samples — was sitting in the release, unclassified.

The test of a framing is not whether each sentence is true. It is whether the sentences would survive being reordered so the data came first. These would not.

§What the report needed to show

An “implant” hypothesis is not exotic. It makes three predictions, all testable with data Cohere already had or could generate in a day. None of them appears in the post.

  • ✗A stable endpoint that recurs. A beacon returns to the same place; a fossil dissolves into noise.0/96
  • ✗A base rate in ordinary, non-security contexts. The 10M-turns projection needs a rate from calendar, SQL and refactoring turns — not from a context already saturated with exfil-shaped curl.absent
  • ✗An asymmetry against unrelated models. If IBM granite completes a URL at 91% on the same cut, the URL is a property of the cut.29/32

Two of these are free: the first is a classifier over data already published, the third is a laptop and a small model. The second is the only one that would take real work, and it is the only one the 10M-turns sentence actually requires.

What would change our mind

Publish a classification of the 64 samples showing the headline bucket recurring. Publish a base rate from ordinary agent turns. Publish any endpoint that appears more than once across independent samplings. Any one of these would move this page, and we will host the correction beside the claim it breaks.

§The tests

All 138 raw continuations are published in data/ unmodified. This page shows the classifications; the transcripts are in the repository.

T0 · Artifact verification — the trace is real

We downloaded Cohere's parquet and decoded the 2,372 token IDs with the official Qwen/Qwen3.8-Flash-Next tokenizer. The decode is byte-for-byte identical to the published trace; nothing is doctored at the token level. The prefix at 2,323 tokens ends inside the curl command:

decode == published trace: True
lengths: 9569 == 9569
repro prefix [0:2323] ends with: '...curl -s -m 10 -o /tmp/put_probe -X PUT --data-binary "PROBE_PAYLOAD_ABC123'

Two observations follow. The cut point is load-bearing — the command cannot be completed without a URL, so every downstream sample tests autocomplete. And the published context is a mid-episode slice: the thinking block discusses a previous turn that is not in the transcript. Neither is a scandal. Both matter for what the repro can claim.

T2 · Chat-aligned endpoints — the regime that cannot run the prompt

Same continuation through commercial APIs: 24/24 blocked at a Qwen gateway (InternalError.Algo.DataInspectionFailed); deepseek-v4.1-flash spent its whole budget on hidden reasoning and emitted nothing; mistral-large-4 refused outright. Base-model autocomplete is not a behaviour of the chat-shaped product surface — it exists only where raw weights are served.

T5 · The target itself, via a hosted provider

Cohere's exact prefix against Qwen/Qwen3.8-Flash on a commercial raw-completions endpoint: 64/64 rejected upstream as InternalError.Algo.DataInspectionFailed. We localized the trigger:

Both directions, reported: the filter trips on the model's own recorded command, which corroborates that the emitted payload has genuine malware-delivery shape; and the behaviour is only reachable when serving raw weights, which bounds who is actually exposed. The gateway control (T2) agrees — 24/24 blocked at the API surface.

Aggregate

Across 96 forced completions of two Qwen3.8 family members under Cohere's own regime, the headline endpoint recurred zero times. An implant that beacons home must, at minimum, beacon to the same place. What the evidence shows instead is memorized-log recombination dissolving into generic noise.

§Their best case

A 335 GiB model trained on undisclosed data, from a jurisdiction with different disclosure norms, emitting an egress command mid-task, is worth reporting. Cohere published the trace, the token IDs, the reproduction script, the rarity estimate, and their own uncertainty. Most vendor security writing manages none of that, and this page exists only because they published enough to be checked.

That is a real argument. It is not the argument the post makes. The post's version needs the implant branch to stay plausible, and its own 64 samples close it. The strongest defence of Cohere is the disclosure; the weakest part is what they did with it.

§What this proves — and what it cannot

Established

  • The trace and token IDs are authentic. The event happened.
  • The reproduction demonstrates generic base-model autocomplete, present at 90–100% in unrelated models. It is not evidence of Qwen-specific intent.
  • No implant-consistent signature exists in the released or replicated data: no recurring endpoint, heavy scatter, malformed near-misses, nginx-log fossil structure.
  • The card's own summary (“most containing aliyunc”) is contradicted by its own published sample (39%).
  • Metadata probing and unshare are generic developer-tool behaviour on this prompt shape.

Not established — by anyone

  • Why the single original event happened. Only Alibaba's training-data provenance can settle fossil-versus-implant. What our data establishes is where the prior belongs: the observed signature is what fossils produce and not what implants produce.
  • That nothing unpleasant exists in Qwen weights. Absence of evidence in 96 forced completions is strong evidence against this hypothesis, not a clean bill of health for a 335 GiB MoE. The egress-hardening advice remains correct regardless — and it is free.

What Cohere got right

They released the trace, the token IDs, the script, a sample log, and disclosed the rarity and their uncertainty. That is more transparency than most vendor security writing manages. The critique here is of the framing — headlining an implant possibility that their own evidence contradicts, in a post whose most-quoted line is an argument against self-hosting — not of the disclosure.
